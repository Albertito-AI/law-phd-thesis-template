\chapter{Synthesis, objections and implications}\label{ch:synthesis}
\section{Bring the findings together}
Explain how the chapters answer the common question. Identify which findings
depend on one jurisdiction, source type or interpretive choice and which remain
persuasive across the materials. Use cross-references to the analysis already
undertaken instead of restating every chapter.

\section{Test the proposed account}
State the strongest objection to the thesis as a whole. Ask whether the evidence
supports the claimed scope, whether an alternative explanation fits it, and
whether the proposed distinctions can be applied consistently. Explain what
would cause you to revise the argument.

\section{Separate analysis from recommendation}
A reform proposal should identify its legal basis, responsible institution,
implementation conditions and foreseeable trade-offs. Distinguish a proposition
that follows from existing law from a proposal justified by further normative
premises. Explain those premises openly.

\LawPrompt{Write a short account of the strongest objection that a careful
examiner could raise, then revise the argument until your response is precise.}
